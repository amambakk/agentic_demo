# Reflection and Self Critique

Reflection is the stage where an agent evaluates its own work before showing it to a user. A critic examines the goal, the plan, and the observations, and produces a verdict plus a list of concrete gaps.

Useful reflection checks are mechanical rather than vague. Did every step in the plan produce an observation? Did any tool fail without a successful retry or fallback? Does the evidence mention every key term from the goal? Were any retrievals graded weak? Is any observation contradicted by another?

Reflection must be bounded. An agent that reflects forever never answers, and each extra loop costs time and money. A budget of two or three replanning cycles is typical, after which the agent answers with whatever it has and states its own uncertainty.

Honest uncertainty is a feature of a good agent. An answer that says which sub-question could not be resolved is more useful than a fluent answer that quietly omits it.

Reflection is also where a failed run becomes a learned lesson: the critic's gap list can be written to episodic memory so the same mistake is anticipated next time.
